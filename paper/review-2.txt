1. The confound cuts both ways, and the paper only applies it in one direction. You correctly argue that shared corpora make the family's collapse uninterpretable as taxonomy structure. But the same logic undermines the claim that the four independent-corpus dimensions are "genuinely distinct": their diagonal-dominance is equally confounded with corpus identity. The privacy encoder scoring ~0.5 on care pairs could mean "privacy is a distinct concept" — or it could mean "the privacy encoder never saw Social-Chem's register and is lost on it." Off-diagonal chance performance is consistent with concept-specificity and with pure domain shift, and the current design can't tell them apart. The paper's own thesis ("specificity tracks corpus independence, not the taxonomy") should make you suspicious of specificity claims too, not just collapse claims. The fix is a matched-register test: evaluate the privacy encoder on privacy-labeled examples written in Social-Chem-style text (concept held constant, register moved), or give two different dimensions corpora from the same family and see whether diagonal-dominance survives. Until then, I'd soften "genuinely distinct from it and from each other" to "distinct at the corpus level, with concept-level distinctness requiring a register-controlled test" — that's the same discipline you applied to the family, pointed the other way.
2. The family pool "beats" claim has a data-quantity confound. The pooled encoder trains on roughly 4× the data of any dedicated encoder, so "pool beats dedicated" (legitimacy +0.124, epistemic +0.071) may partly be sample size, not concept identity. Your core inference only needs "pool ≈ dedicated," which survives; but the beats language invites the confound. Either subsample the pool to dedicated-encoder size, or restate: "matches (and, with 4× data, exceeds)."
3. For rights, separate "unlearnable" from "untransferable." The 9×9 diagonal (0.468) is cross-corpus by construction, and §3.4 says training loss was flat — which is a different failure than transfer failure. If the encoder can't fit even within-corpus ECHR labels, the story is label incoherence or a pipeline problem (case-level violation labels are noisy — a judgment can violate some article; long judgments get diluted by mean-pooling); if within-corpus AUROC is high and only cross fails, that's the framework-relativity signature. The paper needs the within-corpus number for rights (ideally for all nine — the within→cross collapse is part of the story). Right now the most interesting concept-level claim in the paper rests on a result that hasn't been decomposed into its two possible causes.
4. The margin column is internally inconsistent. Spot-checking: most rows report margin against the stricter (higher) null — privacy 0.853−0.55=+0.30, legitimacy 0.708−0.53=+0.18 — but physical_harm reports +0.16, which is the gap to BoW (0.46); against its untrained null (0.50) the margin is +0.12. Rights similarly shows −0.01 (vs BoW) where the binding margin is −0.045 (vs untrained). No verdict flips, but a table where the margin definition silently switches per row is exactly what a hostile reader screenshots. Define margin = cross − max(null) and recompute the column.
5. Report the CIs before circulating, not after. §6 admits pseudo-replication, but with 410–1200 pairs a text-level bootstrap is an afternoon, and several claims sit inside plausible noise bands: the −0.009/−0.011 family-pool gaps (fine — you only need "matches"), but also "legitimacy encoder scores 0.807 on care, higher than care on itself (0.793)" — a 0.014 gap on ~1000 pairs is almost certainly not distinguishable from zero, and the sentence reads as if it is. The qualitative story survives CIs; the specific "higher than itself" flourishes may not.
6. The epistemic attractor deserves a sentence. Everything in the family transfers to epistemic better than to itself (care→epistemic 0.871 > care→care 0.825, etc.). That's a clue, not just a curiosity — most likely epistemic_quality's held-out pairs are simply the easiest (cleanest labels or strongest valence signal), which would also inflate its standalone PASS. A one-line diagnosis (e.g., pair difficulty or label agreement by dimension) would preempt the "your eval difficulty varies by dimension" objection that the transfer matrix otherwise invites.
Smaller things: the title still leads with "a Deontic Transfer Gap," which is the retracted finding — retitle around corpus independence, which is the actual contribution and a better paper anyway. Open questions skip from 1 to 3. And make sure the transfer-matrix and valence-pool scripts are actually committed to the xbse repo before this circulates, since the reproducibility section points there — the zip you sent me earlier predates them.
The successor hypothesis (conduct-level labels transfer; institutionally-mediated entitlement labels don't) is, I agree, stronger and more testable than the column claim — privacy-as-exposure-harm supporting it is a nice observation. The pre-registered falsification for the big open question writes itself: name the independent corpora for care and fairness now (empathy-focused and discrimination-focused datasets exist), predict diagonal-dominance emerges if the concepts are real, and commit that prediction to git before running. Given how this project has gone, that prediction-first move is also your best rhetorical asset: the paper's credibility comes precisely from the fact that every control so far has been allowed to make the story worse.
