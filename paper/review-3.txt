Second-round comments, mostly about §3.5 and the pre-registration, since those are the new load-bearing pieces.

**1. The frozen probe doesn't yet license "coherent, not incoherent."** The within-corpus probe shows rights labels are *predictable within corpus* — which rules out random labels but not artifact-driven ones. A 0.77 probe on ECHR could be reading case topic, article mix, country, or outcome-section phrasing rather than any rights concept; "predictable from corpus-internal confounds" and "coherent concept signal" both produce that number. Two cheap additions would close this: (a) a **within-corpus BoW probe** row in the §3.5 table — if TF-IDF also hits ~0.75 within ECHR, the frozen probe is likely reading surface artifacts, and the "coherent" claim weakens (this matters doubly for the family dimensions, where 0.99+ within Social-Chem is almost certainly judgment-word transparency in the RoTs — you gesture at this but the BoW-within number would make it quantitative); and (b) a **frozen cross-probe** — train the linear probe on corpus A's embeddings, test on B, no fine-tuning. That gives you the same estimator on both sides of the within→cross comparison, fixing the "different estimators, not a clean subtraction" caveat properly instead of caveating it, and if the frozen cross-probe also sits at ~0.5, the transfer failure is confirmed at the representation level rather than the training-dynamics level.

**2. "Largest within→cross drop" needs a defined statistic — and harm is closer than the text implies.** Using the mean of the A/B probe columns: rights drops 0.74→0.475 (≈0.27), but physical_harm drops 0.845→0.622 (≈0.22) — second place, and not by much. Rights is qualitatively distinct because it lands *at chance*, which is the right thing to emphasize; but "largest drop" invites a reviewer to compute exactly what I just did and note that the drop is a continuum, not a cliff. Define the statistic (mean-within minus cross, same estimator per #1b), show the full ranked column, and lean on "only dimension that drops to chance" rather than "largest drop." Interestingly the continuum *helps* your successor hypothesis: harm — conduct-level labels — suffers a big register drop yet stays learnable, while rights — entitlement-level — doesn't survive at all.

**3. There's an unremarked cell in the 9×9 that's both a problem and a gift: `rights→privacy = 0.66`.** It's the largest off-diagonal outside the family block, and nobody in the paper comments on it. I'd bet on a concrete cause: ECHR **Article 8 is the right to private life** — your rights training corpus *contains privacy cases*, so the failed rights encoder partially learned privacy, which is corpus contamination of exactly the kind the paper is about. This is checkable in an afternoon (drop Art-8 cases, re-evaluate rights→privacy) and either outcome is good for you: if the 0.66 vanishes, you've demonstrated your own contamination-detection method catching a live case within your own data — a lovely self-application; if it doesn't, that's a puzzle worth reporting. Either way, leaving the matrix's biggest anomaly unexplained is the kind of thing referees fixate on.

**4. The pre-registered falsifier is too binary — add a manipulation check.** As written: if the family still cross-contaminates under independent corpora, "the ~5-axis collapse is a property of the moral space." But there's a third outcome: the new empathy/discrimination corpora could simply be weak (bad labels, small n, encoders fail to train), producing no diagonal-dominance for reasons that say nothing about moral space. The prereg doc should gate the prediction: the re-corpused care and fairness must first *pass their own cross-dataset gate* on the new pairing; only then is the transfer prediction scored, otherwise the run is "inconclusive — corpus quality," not a falsification. Without that gate, a null result is uninterpretable and the pre-registration loses its teeth.

**5. The register-controlled test has its own confound to pre-empt.** If "privacy-labeled text rewritten in Social-Chem style" means LLM rewriting, the rewrite can launder or destroy the label signal, and a specificity drop would be ambiguous between "was domain shift" and "rewrite erased the concept." Build in a rewrite-fidelity check — the frozen privacy probe should still decode the labels on rewritten text at near its original within-corpus level before the rewritten set is used for the transfer test — and run both directions (family text in privacy register too).
