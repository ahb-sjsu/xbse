But the CI rollout introduced two internal inconsistencies that a referee will treat as red flags precisely because this paper's whole brand is meticulousness, and they both point back at the one issue that's still open. Let me take it in order of importance.

**1. The new CI paragraph contradicts the scorecard table — twice.** Rights is 0.475 in the table (and in §3.4, §3.5, and the abstract) but "0.502 [0.470, 0.533]" in the CI paragraph. Worse, autonomy's point estimate 0.747 lies *outside* its own CI [0.664, 0.732]. A bootstrap CI computed on a fixed model's predictions is roughly centered on the point estimate by construction — a point estimate outside its CI is mathematically impossible under that procedure. So these CIs were evidently computed on *retrained* encoders, not the canonical checkpoints, and the paragraph is inadvertently publishing your retrain variance (autonomy moved ~0.05, rights ~0.03) while presenting itself as eval uncertainty. Two fixes, and you need both: recompute the CIs on the canonical checkpoints' stored predictions so every point estimate sits inside its interval (or, if the re-runs are now canonical, update the table and every downstream number to match — one set of numbers, everywhere); and add a sentence that reports retrain variance *as its own quantity*, because you now have three independent estimates of it (the 0.66→0.60 Art-8 re-measurement, autonomy 0.747→~0.70, rights 0.475→0.502) and it's clearly σ ≈ 0.03–0.06.

**2.
